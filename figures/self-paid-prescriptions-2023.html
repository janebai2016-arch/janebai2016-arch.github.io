Entirely self-paid prescriptions cost Americans $24.0 billion in 2023

Fills where insurance contributed nothing — no Medicare, Medicaid, private, VA, TRICARE or other third-party dollar. They are 31% of all prescription fills and carry half of every out-of-pocket dollar Americans spend on drugs. But the fills and the dollars sit in opposite places.

The nominal rise is an illusion. Consumer prices rose 21.3% over these five years, so 2018's $21.5B is $26.1B in 2023 dollars — meaning real self-paid spending fell about 8%, and total out-of-pocket drug spending fell 19%. What actually grew is the self-paid share: from 45% to 50% of everything patients pay, and from 24% to 31% of fills.

The tail also moved. In 2018 the largest block of self-paid dollars sat in the $68–500 band at 32.8%, with fills over $500 accounting for 18.8%. By 2023 that inverted — the over-$500 band carries 29.5% and $68–500 fell to 18.0%. Spending concentrated further into a smaller group of very expensive fills.

Treat this as two snapshots, not a trend. MEPS reduced its sample after 2020 — 18,145 people in 2018 against 11,858 in 2023 — so the 2023 tail rests on 253 fills where 2018 had 346. Both are thin, and the intervening years would be needed to establish a trajectory.
