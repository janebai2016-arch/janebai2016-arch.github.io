Entirely self-paid prescriptions cost Americans $24.0 billion in 2023

Fills where insurance contributed nothing — no Medicare, Medicaid, private, VA, TRICARE or other third-party dollar. They are 31% of all prescription fills and carry half of every out-of-pocket dollar Americans spend on drugs. But the fills and the dollars sit in opposite places, and the gap widened between 2018 and 2023.

Five years earlier

Self-paid spending rose from $21.5B in 2018 to $24.0B in 2023, and the self-paid share of all prescription fills rose from 24.0% to 31.1% — but the nominal increase is an illusion. Consumer prices rose 21.3% over those five years, so 2018's $21.5B is $26.1B in 2023 dollars, meaning real self-paid spending fell about 8%. Total out-of-pocket drug spending fell 19% in real terms.

What actually grew is the share. Self-paid fills went from 45% to 50% of everything patients pay out of pocket for drugs, and from 24% to 31% of fills. The median self-paid fill barely moved, $10.00 to $10.18. So Americans are not paying more out of pocket in real terms — a larger slice of what they do pay is simply going through no insurance at all, and it is concentrating in a smaller group of very expensive fills. The top-band disproportion rose from 41.7× to 69.6×.

Treat this as two snapshots, not a trend. MEPS reduced its sample after 2020 — 18,145 people in 2018 against 11,858 in 2023 — so the 2023 tail rests on 253 fills where 2018 had 346. Both are thin, and the intervening years would be needed to establish a trajectory.

MEPS 2018 · Prescribed Medicines file HC-206A · 76,873 self-paid fills · 11,141 people
Entirely self-paid is defined as total expenditure above zero, all nine third-party payment sources summing to zero, and self/family payment equal to total expenditure. Spending is weighted by the person weight; fill and person counts are unweighted. Inflation adjustment uses annual-average CPI-U (2018 = 251.107, 2023 = 304.702).
A person with fills in more than one band is counted in each. Hover any band for its underlying counts and dollars in both years.
